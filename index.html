Section A — Short Answer Questions
2 marks each · 3-4 lines kaafi hai · Easy marks mat chhodna!
SA1 Unit 1 What is proper length? (Relativity)
2 marks
▼

Length of a rod measured by an observer who is at REST relative to the rod = Proper Length (L₀). It is the maximum possible length.

L = L₀ √(1 - v²/c²)

Jab rod move karti hai, toh stationary observer ko chhoti dikhti hai — this is Length Contraction. Proper length is always the longest measurement possible.

Simple word mein: apni rod ka size tabhi sahi hai jab tum uske saath khade ho! 😂
SA2 Unit 3 Two independent sources can't produce interference. Why?
2 marks
▼

Two independent sources cannot produce sustained interference because:

  • They are NOT coherent — phase relationship keeps changing randomly
  • Frequencies and wavelengths may differ slightly
  • Phase changes every 10⁻⁸ seconds → fringes keep shifting → no stable pattern
Dono dost alag alag gaana gaayein toh harmony nahi banti — same logic! 🎵
SA3 Unit 2 State Planck's basic postulates of radiation.
2 marks
▼

Postulate 1: Energy of an oscillator is QUANTIZED:

Eₙ = nhν    (n = 1, 2, 3...)

Postulate 2: Energy is emitted/absorbed in discrete packets called photons:

ΔE = Δn · hν

Energy cannot be arbitrary — only specific discrete values are allowed. No continuous emission!

Jaise ATM se sirf 100, 200, 500 wale notes nikalte hain — energy bhi aisi hi "fixed denominations" mein aati hai 💸
SA4 Unit 2 Why is Compton effect NOT observed with visible light?
2 marks
▼

Maximum Compton shift = Δλ_max = 0.0486 Å ≈ 0.05 Å

  • X-rays (λ ≈ 1 Å) → shift is ~5% → easily detectable ✓
  • Visible light (λ ≈ 5000 Å) → shift is only 0.001% → completely undetectable ✗
Note: Effect does exist, it's just too tiny to measure. Exam mein yahi likhna! Zyada mat socho.
Aise samjho: 1 rupee mein 5 paisa change zyada noticeable hai than 5000 rupees mein 5 paisa 😅
SA5 Unit 5 What are nanomaterials? Give properties.
2 marks
▼

Materials with size between 1-100 nm (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m) are called nanomaterials.

Special properties due to small size:

  • Very high surface-to-volume ratio → more reactive
  • Copper (opaque) becomes transparent at nanoscale
  • Gold (solid) becomes liquid at room temperature
  • Aluminum becomes combustible, Platinum becomes a catalyst
Properties itni badal jaati hain ki lagta hai ye alag hi element hai — size really matters bhai! 🔬
SA6 Unit 4 Define acceptance angle, acceptance cone & numerical aperture.
2 marks
▼
  • Acceptance Angle (i_max): Max angle that incident light can make with fiber axis for total internal reflection
  • Acceptance Cone: The cone formed by rotating acceptance angle — any ray within this cone travels through fiber
  • Numerical Aperture:
NA = sin(i_max) = √(n₁² - n₂²)

Higher NA = more light the fiber can capture = better fiber!

NA basically fiber ka "appetite" hai — kitna light khaa sakta hai woh batata hai 😄
SA7 Unit 5 What is superconductivity and critical temperature?
2 marks
▼

Superconductivity: When electrical resistance of a material drops SUDDENLY to ZERO below a certain temperature.

Critical Temperature (Tc): Temperature below which material becomes superconducting.

  • Mercury → Tc = 4.15 K
  • Aluminum → Tc = 1.19 K

Below Tc: resistance = 0  |  Above Tc: normal resistance

Imagine karo — electricity free mein flow ho rahi hai, koi bill nahi! That's superconductivity bhai 🤑
SA8 Unit 2 What is wave-particle duality?
2 marks
▼

EM radiation shows DUAL character:

  • Wave nature: Interference, Diffraction, Polarization
  • Particle nature: Photoelectric effect, Compton effect, Black body radiation

Both properties cannot be observed simultaneously. This is called wave-particle duality.

Light ek jugaadu hai — kabhi wave ban jaati hai, kabhi particle. Physics ka biggest flex! 💪
SA9 Unit 3 Why is Newton's ring centre dark in reflected light?
2 marks
▼

At the point of contact, air film thickness t = 0.

Light reflecting from denser medium (glass) undergoes phase change of π (180°), equivalent to path difference of λ/2.

This causes DESTRUCTIVE interference at centre → centre appears DARK ✓

Contact point pe ekdum zero gap hai, lekin phase change ki wajah se light cancel ho jaati hai — yaar ke against ho jaate ho toh dono zero ho jaate ho 😂
SA10 Unit 4 Differences between single mode and multimode fiber.
2 marks
▼
Single Mode: Core ~5 µm · One path · No dispersion · Long distances · Expensive 💸
Multimode: Core 50-200 µm · Many paths · More dispersion · Short distances · Cheaper

Single mode is better for long-distance communication (telephone networks, internet cables).

Single mode = VIP lane (fast, clean, costly) | Multimode = traffic jam wali road 🚦

Section B — Medium Answer (5 Marks)
1 page each · Formula + brief derivation + conclusion
B1 Unit 1 Time Dilation — Definition + Derivation + Real Effect
5 marks
▼

Definition: A moving clock runs SLOW compared to a stationary clock. This apparent slowing is called Time Dilation.

Derivation: Let proper time in S' = t₀. From inverse Lorentz transform:

t = t₀ / √(1 - v²/c²)

Since √(1-v²/c²) < 1, we get t > t₀ → time appears longer (dilated) to stationary observer.

Real Effect — µ-meson experiment:

  • µ-mesons created 10 km above Earth, speed = 0.998c, lifetime = 2×10⁻⁶ sec
  • Without dilation: distance = 600 m (shouldn't reach Earth)
  • With dilation: t = 3.17×10⁻⁵ sec → distance = 9.5 km ✓ They DO reach Earth!
Exam tip: µ-meson experiment likhna mat bhoolo — 2 marks pakke hain iske liye!
Moving ghadi slow chalti hai — isliye astronauts thoda kam age hote hain. Space travel = cheat code for aging! 🚀
B2 Unit 1 Relativistic Velocity Addition Theorem
5 marks
▼

Setup: Frame S' moves with velocity v w.r.t. S. Particle moves with u' in S'.

From Lorentz inverse transformation, dividing dx by dt:

u = (u' + v) / (1 + u'v/c²)

Consistency check: If u' = c (light speed in S'):

u = (c + v)/(1 + cv/c²) = c(c+v)/(c+v) = c ✓

Speed of light is SAME in all frames — Einstein's 2nd postulate satisfied!

Classic formula — agar light bhaag rahi hai toh tum kuch bhi karo, light ki speed same rahegi. Pakad nahi sakte bhai! 😤
B3 Unit 2 Compton Effect — Derivation of Compton Shift
5 marks
▼

Compton Effect (1921): X-rays scatter off electrons → scattered radiation has GREATER wavelength.

Using conservation of energy and momentum (relativistic):

Δλ = λ' - λ = (h/m₀c)(1 - cosθ) where h/m₀c = 0.0243 Å = Compton wavelength
  • At θ = 90°: Δλ = 0.0243 Å
  • At θ = 180°: Δλ_max = 0.0486 Å (maximum shift)
Remember: Assumptions likhna — single photon + single electron, elastic collision, conservation holds.
Photon ne electron se takkar maari aur thoda slow ho gaya — bilkul aise jaise cycle se car se takkar ke baad tum hi slow ho jaate ho 😭
B4 Unit 2 Particle in 1D Box — Eigen Values & Eigen Functions
5 marks
▼

Particle trapped between x=0 and x=a, V=0 inside, V=∞ outside.

Schrödinger eq inside box: d²ψ/dx² + k²ψ = 0

Applying boundary conditions (ψ=0 at x=0 and x=a):

Eigen Values (Energy): Eₙ = n²h² / (8ma²) n = 1, 2, 3... Eigen Functions: ψₙ = √(2/a) · sin(nπx/a)

Energy is QUANTIZED — particle can only have specific energy values!

n=1 → Ground state | n=2,3... → Excited states

Particle box ke andar bounce kar raha hai aur sirf kuch specific energies allowed hain — jaise flat ke andar room rent fixed hota hai, kuch bhi nahi chalega! 🏠
B5 Unit 3 Fringe Width in Wedge-Shaped Film
5 marks
▼

Two glass surfaces inclined at small angle θ forming a wedge of refractive index µ.

Path difference: Δ = 2µt·cos(r+θ) ± λ/2

Using geometry (t = x·tanθ) and subtracting consecutive fringe positions:

Fringe Width ω = λ / (2µ·sinθ) For air film (µ=1), small θ: ω = λ / (2θ)
  • Fringe width ∝ λ (more wavelength = wider fringes)
  • Fringe width ∝ 1/θ (sharper wedge = narrower fringes)
Jitna zyada angle, utne tight fringes — jaise zoom in/out karte ho photo mein 📸
B6 Unit 3 Fresnel vs Fraunhofer Diffraction — Compare
5 marks
▼
Fresnel: Finite distance · Spherical/cylindrical wavefront · No lens · Zone plates · Centre can be dark
Fraunhofer: Infinite distance · Plane wavefront · Lens needed · Single slit/grating · Centre ALWAYS bright
Exam trick: Fraunhofer mein ALWAYS bright centre hota hai — ye yaad rakh, easy marks!
Fresnel = jugaadu setup, koi lens nahi · Fraunhofer = proper lab setup, sab perfect 🔭
B7 Unit 4 Acceptance Angle & Numerical Aperture — Derivation
5 marks
▼

Core (n₁) + Cladding (n₂), n₁ > n₂. Using Snell's law at fiber end and critical angle condition:

sin(i_max) = √(n₁² - n₂²) / n₀ For air (n₀=1): NA = sin(i_max) = √(n₁² - n₂²)

Acceptance Cone: Full cone of half-angle i_max. Any ray within this cone propagates.

Higher NA = more light accepted = better fiber!

Acceptance angle = fiber ka "welcome zone" — iske bahar wale rays ko bhaga diya jaata hai 🚪
B8 Unit 5 Meissner Effect + Type I vs Type II Superconductors
5 marks
▼

Meissner Effect (1933): When cooled below Tc, superconductor EXPELS all magnetic field from interior. B = 0 inside → perfect diamagnetism (χ = -1).

This CANNOT be explained by zero resistance alone — it's an independent property!

Type I (Soft): One critical field Hc · Complete Meissner · Al, Hg, Zn · Limited use Type II (Hard): Two fields Hc1 & Hc2 · Mixed state exists · Nb-Ti alloy · MRI, Maglev trains
Type II = zyada tolerant hai, thodi magnetic field allow karta hai mixed state mein — real life mein kaam aata hai bhai 🏥

Section C — Long Answer (10 Marks)
2-3 pages each · Full derivations · Diagrams bahut important hain!
C1 Unit 1 Michelson-Morley Experiment — Full Theory + Result + Explanations
10 marks
▼

Objective: Detect Earth's motion through hypothetical ETHER medium.

Setup: Source → Partially silvered plate P at 45° → Mirrors M₁ (perpendicular) & M₂ (along motion) at equal distance l → Telescope T

Draw this diagram! It carries 2-3 marks guaranteed. Label: Source S, plate P, mirrors M₁ M₂, telescope T.

Time Calculations:

Arm parallel to motion: t₁ = (2l/c) · 1/(1-v²/c²) Arm perpendicular: t₂ = (2l/c) · 1/√(1-v²/c²) Expected fringe shift: Δn = 2lv²/(c²λ) ≈ 0.4 fringes

Result: ZERO shift observed! Experiment repeated many times — always ZERO.

Conclusion: Earth's motion through ether CANNOT be detected. Ether concept is WRONG.

Explanations of Negative Result:

  • Ether-Drag Hypothesis: Earth drags ether → no relative motion (later disproved)
  • Fitzgerald-Lorentz Contraction: Moving objects contract by √(1-v²/c²)
  • Einstein's Hypothesis (BEST): Speed of light = constant c in ALL inertial frames → no ether needed → basis of Special Relativity ✓
Poora experiment ether dhoondhne ke liye kiya, mila kuch nahi — aur isi "failure" se Einstein ki duniya badal gayi! 🎉
C2 Unit 1 Einstein's Mass-Energy Relation E = mc² + Energy-Momentum Relation
10 marks
▼

Start with relativistic mass: m = m₀/√(1-v²/c²)

Work done = KE: dK = F·ds = mv·dv + v²·dm

From differentiation: mv·dv = (c²-v²)dm

Substituting: dK = c²·dm

Integrating from m₀ to m:

K = (m - m₀)c² Total Energy = KE + Rest Energy: E = K + m₀c² ∴ E = mc² ← Einstein's Mass-Energy Relation ✓

Physical meaning: Mass and energy are interconvertible! At rest: E₀ = m₀c²

Relativistic Energy-Momentum Relation:

E² = p²c² + m₀²c⁴ ← Very important! For photon (m₀=0): E = pc → photons carry momentum!
E = mc² — duniya ki sabse famous equation, Einstein ne ek formula mein sab bata diya. Nuclear bomb bhi isi se samjhte hain ☢️
C3 Unit 2 Schrödinger Time-Dependent Wave Equation + Physical Significance of Ψ
10 marks
▼

Start with plane wave: Ψ(x,t) = A·e^[-(2πi/h)(Et-px)]

Differentiate twice w.r.t. x → get p². Differentiate once w.r.t. t → get E.

Substitute in total energy E = p²/2m + V:

-(ℏ²/2m) · ∂²Ψ/∂x² + VΨ = iℏ · ∂Ψ/∂t ← Schrödinger Time-Dependent Equation ✓ In 3D: HΨ = EΨ (Hamiltonian form)

Time-Independent form: ∇²ψ + (2m/ℏ²)(E-V)ψ = 0

Physical Significance of Wave Function Ψ:

  • Ψ itself has no direct physical meaning
  • |Ψ|² = probability density (Max Born's interpretation)
  • |Ψ|² dτ = probability of finding particle in volume element dτ
  • Normalization: ∫|Ψ|²dV = 1 (particle must be SOMEWHERE)
  • Ψ must be: single-valued, finite, continuous, normalizable
Ψ khud kuch nahi batata, |Ψ|² batata hai "particle kahan milega ka chance" — quantum mein sab probability hai bhai, certainty kuch nahi! 🎲
C4 Unit 3 Newton's Rings — Theory + Ring Diameter Derivations
10 marks
▼

Setup: Plano-convex lens on flat glass plate. Monochromatic light → interference rings seen in microscope.

Why circular rings? Air film has same thickness in all directions at same distance → circular symmetry ✓

Centre is DARK because at t=0, path diff = λ/2 (due to phase change) → destructive ✓

Geometry: r² = 2Rt (from Pythagoras, t << R)

Bright Rings: Dₙ = √[2(2n-1)λR] ∝ √(odd natural numbers) D ∝ √1, √3, √5, √7 ... Dark Rings: Dₙ = √(4nλR) ∝ √n D ∝ √1, √2, √3, √4 ...

Application: Find wavelength of light or radius of curvature:

R = Dₙ² / (4nλ)
Newton ne dekha soap bubble pe rings aur physics nikaal di — aise hi Newton hote hain! Humne bus padhni hai 😭
C5 Unit 3 Fraunhofer Diffraction at Single Slit + Prove I₁ = 4.5% of I_max
10 marks
▼

Parallel light → slit (width e) → diffracted → screen

Let total phase = 2α, then resultant amplitude:

R = A · sinα/α → I = A² · sin²α/α²

Minima (I=0): sinα = 0 → α = ±mπ → e·sinθ = ±mλ (m=1,2,3...)

Maxima: α = tanα → solutions at α = 0, ±3π/2, ±5π/2...

At α=0 (Principal max): I_max = A² At α=3π/2 (1st secondary max): I₁ = A² · [sin(3π/2)/(3π/2)]² = A² · 4/(9π²) Ratio: I₁/I_max = 4/(9π²) ≈ 0.045 = 4.5% ✓

Width of central maximum = 2λD/e (twice any secondary max)

Exam guaranteed: "Prove I₁ = 4.5% of I_max" — ye question almost har saal aata hai!
Central band sabse bright aur bada hota hai — baki sab secondary hain, jaise class mein ek topper baki sab average 😂
C6 Unit 4 Ruby Laser — Construction, Working, Energy Levels, Drawbacks
10 marks
▼

First laser by Maiman (1960) — solid state, pulsed, 3-level laser system

Active Material: Cr³⁺ (Chromium) ions in Al₂O₃ crystal doped with 0.05% Cr₂O₃

Construction:

  • Pink ruby rod (cylindrical, 2-20 cm long)
  • One end: fully silvered (total reflector)
  • Other end: partially silvered (output window)
  • Surrounded by helical xenon flash tube (optical pump)
Draw: Ruby rod in center, flash tube coiled around, silvered ends, laser output arrow. 3 marks for diagram!

Working (4 steps):

  • Pumping E₁→E₃: Flash tube emits 5500 Å green light → Cr³⁺ absorbs → jumps to E₃
  • Radiationless E₃→E₂: Quick fall to metastable E₂ (lifetime 10⁻³ s), heat given to lattice
  • Population Inversion: E₂ accumulates ions → inversion between E₂ and E₁
  • Stimulated Emission E₂→E₁: Spontaneous photon triggers identical 6943 Å photons → laser output

Drawbacks:

  • Very HIGH pumping power needed (>50% atoms must be excited)
  • Only green component of pump light is useful → low efficiency
  • Output is PULSED (not continuous)
  • Generates heat affecting performance
3-level laser mein ground state se excited state pump karna padta hai — zyada mehnat! 4-level laser zyada efficient hoti hai, woh alag question hai 😅
C7 Unit 5 Superconductivity — Meissner Effect, Type I vs II, Critical Field
10 marks
▼

Below Tc, resistance drops to ZERO suddenly. Different Tc values:

Hg→4.15K · Pb→7.2K · Nb₃Al→17.5K Critical Field Formula: Hc(T) = Hc(0)[1 - (T/Tc)²]

At T=0: max Hc. At T=Tc: Hc=0. Graph is parabolic.

Meissner Effect: Below Tc, superconductor EXPELS all magnetic flux → B=0 inside → perfect diamagnet (χ=-1)

Two independent properties of superconductors:

  • Zero resistance (R=0)
  • Perfect diamagnetism (Meissner effect) — these are SEPARATE!
Type I (Soft): One Hc, complete Meissner, Al/Zn/Hg, limited apps Type II (Hard): Two fields Hc1 & Hc2, mixed vortex state, Nb-Ti alloy, MRI/Maglev
Draw: B vs H graph for Type I and Type II — shows complete and partial Meissner effect.
Type II wale MRI machine mein kaam aate hain — isiliye doctor itne expensive tests charge karte hain! Superconductor ki wajah se 😂
C8 Unit 5 Top-Down & Bottom-Up Approaches + CVD Method for Nanomaterials
10 marks
▼

Nanomaterials: At least one dimension in 1-100 nm range.

1. Top-Down Approach: Start with bulk → break DOWN to nanoscale

  • Techniques: Lithography, ball milling, etching, laser ablation
  • Analogy: Carving a statue from rock
  • Disadvantage: Non-uniform size, may introduce defects

2. Bottom-Up Approach: Build atom by atom UP from smallest units

  • Techniques: CVD, Sol-Gel, chemical synthesis
  • Analogy: Building a wall brick by brick
  • Advantage: Better control of size and structure

CVD (Chemical Vapor Deposition) — Bottom-Up:

Steps: 1. Substrate + metal catalyst (Ni/Co/Fe) 2. Heat to ~700°C 3. Blow CH₄ (carbon source) + NH₃/H₂ (process gas) 4. High temp breaks C-H bonds → free carbon atoms 5. Carbon bonds with catalyst → forms nanotubes (CNT)

Produces SWNT (single wall) and MWNT (multi wall) carbon nanotubes.

Quantum Structures: Quantum Well (1D reduced) | Quantum Wire (2D) | Quantum Dot (3D)

Draw CVD setup: Quartz tube + gas inlet/outlet + sample on quartz boat + oven. 2-3 marks!
Bottom-up = Minecraft style building 🧱 | Top-down = Minecraft mein TNT se blast karna 💥 — physics mein dono kaam aate hain!
